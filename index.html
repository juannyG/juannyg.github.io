I've been building software for twenty years, sixteen of them at Ordergroove, where I joined as one of the first engineers when the whole company was thirteen people.

About

I'm a software engineer and architect who likes the whole arc of a system: sketching it, building it, scaling it, and keeping it calm at three in the morning. These days that mostly means APIs, identity, and security: deciding how systems talk to each other, who they trust, and why.

I care as much about how teams work as how code works. I've spent years leading a team split between New York and Argentina from my home in Vermont, and I believe good engineering comes from clear thinking, shared ownership, and people who look out for each other.

This year I finished my M.S. in Computer Science at Columbia Engineering, studying part time and remotely while working full time, twenty years after my B.S. from the same school. Away from the keyboard, you'll find me playing music, cooking, or reading about philosophy, psychology, and politics.

Graduate work

  • Distributed training on commodity hardware

    A study of how data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism and DeepSpeed ZeRO actually perform on PCIe-connected GPUs without NVLink, across models from 10M to 1B parameters. We found that only data parallelism and ZeRO Stage 1 scaled meaningfully on two GPUs, and that communication overwhelmed every strategy at four. Written with Can Kerem Akbulut and Rakene Chowdhury for Scaling LLMs: Systems, Optimization, and Emerging Paradigms, Fall 2025.
